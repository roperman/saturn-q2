Quake 2 on the Sega Saturn · the engine · October 2026

What happens each frame, and each tick

The game runs as a loop of frames, 25 a second on PAL and up to 30 on NTSC, and inside it Quake 2's game ticks at its own 10 a second. Every decision below is a branch in the code: the box under each says where. The Saturn Frame Timeline shows the same frame against the clock.

How it nests

Each name opens its chart. The frame calls the game step when the game runs; the game step runs the ticks that are due; a monster's tick runs the AI of its animation's frame.

The frame

Everything starts here. The coloured edge says which processor does a step when it isn't the master.

Alongside: the slave and the DSP

Both run their own loops, started by the master's signals. Neither has to be waited for in the usual frame.

The game step

game_step, src/main.c; g_frame, src/g_main.c

Quake 2's game thinks in tenths of a second. The frames don't fall on tenths, so each clock collects the frames' time and runs a tick for every tenth it has. The monsters are split into eight groups whose tenths are spread across the tick, so a frame does an even share of them instead of all of them every fourth frame.

The world tick

g_tick, src/g_main.c

Everything that isn't a monster's AI: triggers, pickups, and the entities waiting to do something later.

A monster tick

g_tick_monsters, src/g_main.c

One group's monsters, every eighth entity. Most of a fight's time goes in the AI it calls and the traces that does.

The animation frame

M_MoveFrame, src/g_ai.c

A monster is always in a move: a run of animation frames from its m_*.c table, each with an AI function, how far it steps, and an extra (a shot, a swing, a sound). The move's end function picks what comes next. That table is the monster: the gunner's says when its chaingun fires, the parasite's when its tongue bites.

Chasing

ai_run, src/g_ai.c

The other two

ai_charge: turn to face the enemy, and step if the frame says to. ai_move: step straight ahead by the frame's distance (pains, deaths, a flyer backing off as it fires).

Deciding to attack

ai_checkattack, M_CheckAttack, src/g_ai.c

Asked every frame of a chase. One trace answers both "can it see you" and "is the shot clear". A monster that decides to attack turns first and starts its attack move once it's facing you.

Moving

M_MoveToGoal, SV_NewChaseDir, SV_StepDirection, SV_movestep, SV_flystep, src/g_ai.c

Quake's monsters don't path-find: each tick they step towards their goal, and when a step is blocked they try other directions in a fixed order. A step is a box trace through the level, which is why a crowd of blocked monsters is the dearest thing in a fight.

Outside the ticks: hits, pain, death, waking up

g_damage, src/g_main.c; FoundTarget, src/g_ai.c

A hit is handled the moment it lands, whoever's tick or frame it lands in: your hitscan shots in the game step, projectiles and explosions in the effects, the monsters' blows in their ticks.

A monster that sees you

FindTarget makes it the tick's "sight entity": any other monster that can see it this tick wakes too and comes for you, unless it's set to ambush.

A noise you make

Firing makes you the tick's "sound entity". A monster within 1,000 units hears it, turns towards it and goes to look, through walls; an ambusher must also see you.

A trigger

Some monsters aren't there until a trigger names them (the flyers on Installation and Comm Center among them): they appear, and come for whoever set it off. Others are told to attack by a trigger's target.

Being hit

A monster you hurt turns on you at once, whatever it was doing, and its pain move plays at most every three seconds.
