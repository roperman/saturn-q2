Quake 2 on the Sega Saturn · one frame · October 2026

What runs where, and when

The Saturn has two SH-2 CPUs, a DSP and a DMA engine in its system control unit, two video chips and a 68000 for sound. The port keeps them busy at once. The chart follows an average frame of the fight benchmark on PAL (25 frames a second, so 40 ms each), to scale. While the CPUs build frame N, VDP1 draws frame N−1 and the TV shows frame N−2. The Saturn Engine Flowchart has what each step decides.

frame N the frames before and after waiting the swap: timer interrupt at line 216, then the vblank

From the fight benchmark in Mednafen (502 frames, every one two fields): the CPUs busy 37.3 ms of each 40. The master: the pad 0.2 ms, set-up 0.5, the BSP walk 10.0, the game's tick 11.4 (on average a frame; 18.0 at most), drawing from the back 14.7. The slave draws for 32.9 ms (it waits on the walk now and then), and makes the next frame's move at the end. The DSP's parts and VDP1's time are estimates; the texture maker costs the CPUs 27 µs a frame here. On a phone, scroll the chart sideways.

Frame N, in order

The master SH-2 runs the loop in src/main.c. Everything else is started by it, or runs on its own clock.

  1. 0.0 ms
    MasterTakes the move the Slave made for this frame at the end of the last one: the pad it read, the doors and lifts moved, you moved (Quake 2's pmove, against the level's brushes).
  2. 0.0
    MasterStarts the Slave on the monsters' list: which are in view and where, their light, and the dynamic lights on them.
  3. 0.2
    MasterCamera, the gun in your hands, the sky's scroll, the effects (projectiles move and hit, flashes fade; this frame's lights, sprites and beams), the screen's tint.
  4. 0.7
    MasterStarts the DSP on every visible model's vertices; it runs its other programs after them on its own. The DMA fetches the gun's next animation frames from the RAM cart.
  5. 0.7
    MasterWalks the BSP tree front to back (walk.s), publishing the visible faces as it finds them. The slave starts drawing the first ones straight away.
  6. 1.4 → 36.8
    SlaveDraws from the front of the list: each face's setup, grid and cells in assembly (face.s, grid.s, cells.s) into VDP1 commands, and each model once the DSP has its vertices.
  7. 10.7
    MasterRuns the game's tick while the slave draws, when one's due: the world's 10 Hz tick and the monsters in eight groups, with their traces through the level (g_main.c, g_ai.c, trace.c).
  8. 22.1
    MasterDraws the gun, then faces and models from the back of the list, until it meets the slave coming the other way.
  9. 36.8
    MasterTells the DSP's texture maker the frame's done and makes any texture it hadn't got to. The textures the CPUs needed go across by DMA, then the status bar goes on top.
  10. 36.8
    SlaveReads the pad and makes the next frame's move while the master finishes, so the next frame starts with it done.
  11. 37.3
    MasterHands the frame over: the DMA copies both CPUs' command lists and Gouraud tables into VDP1's memory, and the list waits for the swap.
  12. 39.5 / 40
    VDP1A timer interrupt at line 216 checks VDP1 has finished the frame before. If it has, VDP1 starts on frame N at the vblank, and the buffer it just finished (frame N−1) goes on screen.
  13. 40 → ~62
    VDP1Draws frame N's thousand or so textured quads and polygons into the back buffer, while the CPUs are already on frame N+1.
  14. 80
    VDP2Frame N reaches the screen: VDP2 shows VDP1's buffer over its own sky layer and gradient, with the colour offset for underwater tints and flashes.

The DSP's frame

The DSP holds one 256-word program at a time. Each of these loads the next itself, by DMA from the RAM cart, so the CPUs start it once a frame and never wait for the chain.

Why it's arranged like this

Two frames in flight

VDP1 draws one frame while the CPUs build the next, so neither waits for the other. The price is latency: a frame reaches the screen about two frames after the pads were read.

The walk feeds the slave

Faces are published one at a time as the walk finds them, so the slave doesn't wait for the whole list. The master takes from the back and the slave from the front, and they meet wherever the work runs out.

The game runs during the drawing

Monsters and their traces run on the master while the slave draws. The slave takes more of the list that frame, which balances the two CPUs.

The next move, made early

The slave finishes drawing before the master, so it reads the pad and moves you for the next frame then. The next frame starts with that done, about 1.5 ms sooner.

The DSP keeps itself busy

Its programs load each other from the cart, so after one start a frame it works through the models, the walls' lights and the textures with no CPU attention.

Everything else runs alongside

The DMA, the sound CPU and the SMPC each run on their own. The CPUs only hand them work and collect the results, and never wait for them in the common case.
